## Episode promise and order

Working episode thesis: **four families demand access, forgiveness or unpaid
care after ignoring the harm they caused**.

The order is deliberate:

1. Start with the clearest betrayal and strongest two-second package.
2. Change the conflict from infidelity to personal safety.
3. Move from one relationship to systemic parentification.
4. Finish on the most visually escalating pattern: 17 years of silence → 12
   calls a day → repeated police welfare checks.

Cold open draft:

> Моя сестра забеременела от моего мужа. Я подала на развод, но теперь вся
> семья злится не на них — а на меня, потому что я отказываюсь её прощать.

Required disclosure, once:

> Это личные рассказы пользователей Reddit; независимо они не подтверждены.

### Story 1 — sister / husband / pregnancy

Source: `r/relationship_advice` post `1uw7804`, author
`ThrowRAMPerspective`, body SHA-256
`c895b8117633b5192c1ce9f16c539c93bbc069f31bb34df5b80460ab0a5a85d6`.

1. A 27-year-old woman learns that her 29-year-old sister slept with her
   28-year-old husband and became pregnant.
2. She throws him out, files for divorce and refuses reconciliation.
3. The betrayal is sharpened by fertility treatment: the couple had been
   exploring options because the narrator had difficulty becoming pregnant,
   and the sister knew this.
4. Parents and brothers arrange an unwanted meeting so the sister can
   apologize. The narrator refuses and demands distance.
5. Her mother centers the sister's stress and pregnancy; a brother says he
   understands but admits he would side with the rest of the family.
6. Her father says the sister is already suffering because the ex-husband
   wants nothing to do with her or the baby.
7. Her mother invokes sisterhood and asks about the baby's future. She later
   asks the narrator to help obtain child support from the ex-husband.
8. The narrator ignores most family messages, focuses on the divorce and asks
   whether the remaining family relationship can or should be saved.

Do not soften the cruelty of the conflict, but do not turn the narrator's angry
statements into endorsement or invented threats.

### Story 2 — friend / controlling boyfriend / homophobia

Source: `r/offmychest` post `1v0l1ei`, author `isnt_THIS_crazy`, body
SHA-256 `fe5d2c7d646a9db6ea12995067814da5b34e3fb67d5dfbfed3da9b2d4996c566`.

1. A 39-year-old gay man has been close friends with a 38-year-old woman for
   24 years; her parents are like a second family to him.
2. She meets a 29-year-old man on TikTok. Within months he transfers jobs and
   moves directly into her home.
3. He uses a flight-tracking pretext to introduce location tracking and later
   monitors and punishes her social behavior.
4. After being asked to leave a prayer/Freemason group, he says they should
   instead live according to his reading of the Bible and that gay people
   should be stoned to death.
5. The narrator refuses to meet someone who believes he deserves death.
6. His friend minimizes it: he supposedly meant gay people generally, not the
   narrator personally, and frames the statement as religious doctrine.
7. The narrator treats the meeting as both degrading and potentially unsafe,
   and asks why friendship should require him to pretend otherwise.

Keep the safety framing and exact distinction between reported speech and
verified fact. Do not visually quote or glorify violent language.

### Story 3 — parentification / more than ten children

Source: `r/relationship_advice` post `1uy2j23`, author
`ThrowRATempturesangs`, body SHA-256
`ea722be82d35425968885a25e6ce47414eb60dbcbb942bf7ae3ed5d3ec0a02de`.

1. A 20-year-old man is the oldest in a deeply religious family with more than
   ten children; his parents reject birth control even when pregnancy creates
   medical risk.
2. His father does not earn enough for the household, his mother does not carry
   the parenting load, and most sibling care is assigned to him.
3. Refusal brings harsh punishment. His father simultaneously calls caregiving
   unmanly and relies on him because the younger boys are not required to help.
4. The burden damages his education; he leaves high school during his junior
   year with his parents' support.
5. At 18 he leaves both the church and the home and stops providing unpaid care.
6. With nobody effectively raising the children, both parents demand that he
   return because he is needed to keep the family running.
7. His mother is pregnant again, and even some siblings pressure him to come
   back. He feels guilt but does not want contact or another cycle of parenting.
8. He plans eventually to move farther away and asks how to handle the family
   while he lacks access to therapy and is not ready to return to school.

Avoid broad attacks on religion. The conflict is the reported coercion,
parentification and educational harm in this specific family.

### Story 4 — 17 years of silence / repeated calls and police checks

Source: `r/AmItheAsshole` post `1uviexk`, author
`Opposite-Meringue610`, body SHA-256
`e1d05f200d4f84db3be4b50a900d67f44b798b34649a496d5842e254f9340f60`.

1. The narrator's adult stepdaughter once messaged the narrator's friends,
   calling her abusive despite only limited, polite contact.
2. After a dispute following her brother's death, the daughter cuts off her
   father, stepmother and younger half-sister for 17 years.
3. Her father sends messages and letters for years, then accepts the estrangement.
4. She unexpectedly returns, initially warm, then calls with changing accounts
   of health, money, marriage and childhood problems.
5. Calls grow to as many as 12 per day. When calls are unanswered, she repeatedly
   sends police to the couple's home for welfare checks.
6. She accuses the narrator of blocking access to her father and sends threats;
   the narrator says she always tells her husband when his daughter calls.
7. The husband interprets the behavior as care and calls his wife cruel and
   disrespectful for refusing forgiveness or direct contact.
8. The narrator asks whether maintaining her boundary makes her wrong.

Preserve uncertainty: these are the narrator's allegations. The brother's death
is context, not visual spectacle.
